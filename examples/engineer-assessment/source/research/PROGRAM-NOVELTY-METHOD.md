# Novelty method for the whole assessment research program

Status: **candidate research method**, 2026-10-27. Current rule set: `ea.program-novelty-rules/0.4` (`NR-01`–`NR-07`; v0.2 clarified purpose/evaluation facets and domain transfer, v0.3 added cross-layer interaction and circular-evidence limits, v0.4 separates research-workflow changes from study-design changes). The frozen v1–v5 review packets retain `ea.program-novelty-rules/0.3`; they are not silently reinterpreted under v0.4. This method covers case generation, simulator design, response-process hypotheses, scoring models, evaluation methods, research workflows and product experiments. “Novel” always means *different from a named reference set for a named purpose*. It never means globally first, scientifically established or ready for employment use. A changed rule meaning requires a new rule-set version; past reviews retain the version they actually applied.

## The unit of novelty

Every candidate contribution must name one unit and its comparison boundary. We must not add up unrelated novelties into a single number.

| Unit | What can actually be new | Required comparator | Main false novelty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research hypothesis | A distinct, falsifiable prediction about an observable relationship or boundary condition | Prior local hypotheses and relevant published findings | Rewording a familiar expectation or saying “AI improves quality” without an outcome, baseline or failure condition |
| Simulator mechanism | A causal/behavioral rule that changes an observable decision or revision sequence and improves a predeclared human-alignment target | Neutral LLM, existing persona prompts, simple statistical baseline and previous simulator version | A detailed fictional biography that only changes voice or word choice |
| Case mechanism | A different dependency, evidence pattern, solution grammar or revision demand | Frozen bank, rejected drafts, ordinary examples and public exposure leads | New domain nouns, changed story, embedding distance or a famous riddle with fresh wording |
| Scoring model | A new evidence extraction, missingness, uncertainty or decision rule that improves a predeclared validity or reliability target | Existing anchored rubric, blind human ratings and simple score baselines | Renamed dimensions, more precise looking weights or better fit to synthetic answers |
| Evaluator/stress test | A fixture or test that exposes a distinct failure class that current checks miss | Existing fixtures, known failure modes and reviewer disagreement log | More generated examples of the same error without new detection power |
| Research workflow | A repeatable search, review, selection or execution operation that changes which uncertainties are resolved per total research cost | Existing local workflow, simple generic-creativity or ordinary-ideation instruction, and relevant published methods | Renaming an existing operator, adding a form field that is not applied, or producing more polished ideas without executed value |
| Study design | A contrast that identifies a previously confounded effect or tests a decisive rival explanation | Existing experiment and its identifiability audit | Larger sample of the same confounded design |
| Product feedback | A form or explanation that helps a user act on evidence without exaggerating its certainty | Current report and observed comprehension/action outcomes | A more attractive score display without more accurate understanding |

For each unit, write: `claim_id`, object/version, scope, dated reference corpus, closest prior work, mechanism delta, predicted observable delta, rival explanation, smallest discriminating test, useful outcome, negative outcome, uncertainty, owner decision and evidence links. The first four fields prevent duplicate ideas being retitled; the test fields distinguish an hypothesis from an aspiration.

A **combination** of known case, simulator and scorer operations is a separate research object only if it predicts an interaction or decision outcome that its parts do not. Specify a factorial or removal ablation: each component alone, the combined system and the ordinary baseline on the same source and budget. If the combined gain is explained by one existing component, record an integration refinement rather than a new program mechanism. Changing all layers at once and observing a better final score cannot identify the cause.

A **research workflow** changes how ideas are found, filtered, challenged, tested or selected; a **study design** changes which effect a particular experiment can identify. For example, requiring a failure map plus closest-prior challenge is a workflow candidate. E12's O/A/B/A+B assignment is a study-design candidate for testing that workflow. The workflow needs matched-budget, source-balanced executed-decision evidence before it can be called a useful local addition; the study needs an identifiability audit. The same result cannot certify both merely because it appears in one report.

An AI closed loop can produce mutually confirming case, answer, score and reviewer artifacts because the same model family or prompt conventions shaped each stage. Preserve model/author lineage and compare against independently constructed controls, blind domain reviewers and eventually permissioned developer evidence. Agreement among synthetic outputs is development evidence at most; it cannot promote a human-alignment, construct-validity or hiring claim.

## Candidate decision rule

This is a staged judgment, not an automatically calculated “novelty score.”

1. **Source closure:** pin the local baseline, exact literature search date/query/source list, relevant prior failures and version of the object. An empty exposure corpus is `unknown`, never `novel`.
2. **Difference:** identify the smallest operative change in purpose, mechanism or evaluation. Classify `same`, `surface_variant`, `mechanism_variant`, `new_mechanism_candidate`, or `unresolved`. Record the exact baseline feature that changed. A new domain can matter when it changes a real constraint, evidence opportunity or owner decision; a changed story noun alone is a surface variant. Similarity search supplies leads; independent reviewers decide semantic equivalence.
3. **Discriminating prediction:** name at least one observation on which the candidate and its closest rival disagree. If both predict the same answer pattern or score, the candidate has not yet yielded a testable research addition.
4. **Usefulness:** specify which decision improves: finding case defects, matching human response paths, recovering valid unusual answers, reducing rater error, improving independent outcome prediction, reducing unfairness, or improving participant comprehension. Novelty without a plausible useful consequence remains an exploratory idea.
5. **Feasibility and risk:** check answerability, ten-minute burden, access mode, cost, contamination/leakage, consent and role relevance. A model that improves a synthetic metric but makes participant scoring less fair cannot be promoted by its novelty label.
6. **Evidence level:** `idea`, `reviewed candidate`, `synthetic contrast`, `permissioned human pilot`, `independent holdout replication`, or `deployment finding`. Record negative and no-opening results at the same level as positive ones. A new idea does not inherit validity from its parent.

Require an **execution check** for a research hypothesis before calling it a useful local addition: did its experiment actually resolve a decision, expose a failure, or improve a predeclared model on held-out evidence? An attractive novel proposal that cannot be implemented or whose test is confounded remains an idea. A failed hypothesis can still be useful when its controlled rejection closes a real uncertainty.

The decision is `duplicate`, `hold for ambiguity`, `test candidate`, `useful local addition`, or `rejected after test`, always with scope. “Useful local addition” requires a controlled comparator and observed advantage on its stated outcome; it is not a claim of academic priority or hiring validity. Promotion to product or score policy has a separate owner and evidence gate.

## Versioned rule set for every research object

These are **review rules**, not a model that autonomously certifies originality. Apply `NR-01`–`NR-07` to each hypothesis, simulator mechanism, scoring rule, case, evaluator fixture, research workflow, study design or feedback change. Record `pass`, `fail` or `unknown` with a short reason, source IDs, reviewer and object version; never turn `unknown` into `pass` by default. A research object can be kept for exploration after a failed rule, but its novelty/usefulness label must reflect the failure. Rules must be versioned and applied to old and new candidates in the same comparison cut.

| Rule | Question and evidence | Effect of a failed or unknown check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `NR-01` reference closure | Is the local baseline, closest external prior, dated search scope and exact object version named? | `unknown`: hold novelty judgment; lack of a search hit is not priority evidence. |
| `NR-02` operative difference | Which mechanism, dependency, evidence opportunity, decision rule or observable prediction changes after removing domain nouns, style and naming? | If none: duplicate or surface variant. If unresolved: blinded semantic review. |
| `NR-03` discriminating prediction | What observation would favor this candidate over its closest rival under the same case/data/cost? | If none: research question or design idea only, not a testable hypothesis. |
| `NR-04` evidence and reasoning chain | Does each source actually support the premise used to derive the prediction, are contrary results recorded, and is the evidence independent enough for the stated claim? | Unsupported bridge or circular AI agreement: repair or restrict the claim. A citation list alone is not a reasoning chain. |
| `NR-05` executable contrast | Can the smallest test produce different interpretable outcomes without leakage, confounding or an unobservable target? | Hold until a feasible contrast is specified; larger synthetic sample does not repair identifiability. |
| `NR-06` useful effect and harms | Does the change improve a declared decision at matched effort while preserving valid rare paths, case answerability, access and fairness? | A positive novelty judgment may coexist with `not useful` or `harmful`; do not promote. |
| `NR-07` execution and replication | Did a controlled run resolve the declared uncertainty, and did a second cut support any broad generalization? | Without a run: candidate only. Without replication: restrict scope to the observed cut. |

For `NR-04`, [HypER](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1292/) shows why reasoning-chain validity deserves separate evaluation from final idea appeal. [SciMON](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.18/) already uses retrieval and iterative comparison with prior work, and [Shahid et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.sdp-1.9/) test purpose/mechanism/evaluation facets under a bounded retrieved literature set, so our source-relative critique is an adaptation, not a claim to invent that approach. A polished protocol is insufficient evidence of feasible impact; [Mohammadi et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-short.50/) found outcome evidence more reliable than experiment description alone for the feasibility judgments they studied. These findings motivate the rules; their datasets do not validate our rule set.

## Worked contrasts across the program

| Proposed idea | Novelty judgment now | Discriminating test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Put a hot account limit in a different business story | Surface variant if the same hot-key race, invariant and remedy remain | Blind mechanism reviewers compare the state transitions and valid solution paths. |
| Give a persona a different language and favorite domain | Unresolved simulator effect; not a new mechanism by itself | Hold case/model/tools constant; compare operative paths and style against neutral and repeated same-condition draws. |
| Add a `partial-but-valid` state before final answer | New simulator-mechanism candidate if it predicts different reveal revision or uncertainty patterns | Compare held-out human transition frequencies with and without this state, alongside an unprofiled baseline. |
| Increase the creativity weight in a composite score | Weight variant; no demonstrated scoring addition | Freeze human-rated evidence and independent outcome; test calibration, ranking and subgroup error on untouched data. |
| Test a fluent wrong answer with perfect terminology | Potential useful evaluator fixture if existing evaluator awards unjustified credit | Show a reproducible false positive before repair and its elimination afterward without harming unusual valid answers. |
| Cross persona prompts with case families and response mode instead of one case | Study-design candidate that may resolve existing confounding | Show that each planned contrast is identifiable; then estimate interaction and replicate on held-out family. |

## Hypothesis generation loop

Start from a **failure ledger**, not from a request for many ideas. For each observed failure, produce a causal map: source condition → response or scoring mechanism → visible failure → alternative cause. Generate at least three types of rival: (a) a simulator or scoring mechanism change, (b) a case/administration explanation, and (c) a measurement artifact such as rater or ASR error. Each rival must predict something different under one controlled intervention. Retain the ordinary baseline and rejected ideas in the ledger so repeated rediscovery is visible.

For simulation, use behavior units rather than personality labels: first operation, alternative generation, evidence request, uncertainty statement, commitment, reveal revision and abandonment. A persona prompt is an experimental treatment; its intended action is only observed if a blind coder finds it in the answer. The [effect protocol](simulation/PERSONA-ANSWER-EFFECT-MEASUREMENT.md) states separate estimands for manipulation uptake, path, quality, revision and style. A simulator can be better for failure discovery while still failing to match human frequencies; report those purposes separately.

For scoring, generate hypotheses from **disagreement cases**: an unusual valid answer, a confident but unsupported answer, a concise complete answer, missing ASR evidence, an apparently cooperative message with no executable commitment. Propose the smallest rule change that resolves one failure and explicitly test whether it creates a new false positive or false negative elsewhere. Only permissioned developer data and independent outcomes can support a claim that a score measures engineering capability or selection utility.

## Research basis and boundary

[QGEval](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.658/) found that question-generation quality needs multiple dimensions and that automatic metrics can disagree with human judgments, especially on answerability and answer consistency. We adapt that as a reason to keep novelty, answerability and usefulness separate; QGEval did not validate this assessment. [Beaty and Johnson's SemDis study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32869137/) shows that semantic distance can help study verbal creativity in its tasks; it does not make semantic distance a sufficient score for our multi-step case answers. [Luchini et al.'s engineering design study](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/automating-creativity-assessment-in-engineering-design-a-psychome/) combined expert content review, student administration and expert response ratings for generated items. Its classroom setting supports the need for human validation here, not the transfer of its item statistics to hiring.

[Hu and Collier](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.554/) found limited explanatory contribution from persona variables in studied subjective annotation datasets. [Araujo et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1364/) found expert-persona performance sensitivity to irrelevant prompt details across their tasks. These studies motivate controlled persona ablations and style/content separation; neither gives an effect size for our cases. [Säuberli et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.bea-1.21/) found weak enough human item-response correspondence to reject zero-shot LLMs as educational assessment pilot substitutes in their setting. The human bridge here must be measured on our own permissioned developer responses and held-out cases.

[Si, Yang and Hashimoto's blinded ideation study](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ea94957d81b1c1caf87ef5319fa6b467-Abstract-Conference.html) found LLM ideas rated more novel but somewhat less feasible than expert ideas in their NLP setting. Their [execution follow-up](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803) found the apparent advantage shrank when randomly assigned researchers implemented ideas and experts reviewed the work. This directly motivates keeping proposal novelty separate from executed research value; it does not quantify novelty of our hypotheses. [Wang et al.'s software-engineering judge study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06193) found judge alignment varied by task and method, which motivates case-specific scorer validation instead of promoting a generic LLM score from fluent synthetic examples.
